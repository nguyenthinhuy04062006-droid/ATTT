## 1. Architectural Overview and State Matrix Representation
AES-128 is a symmetric block cipher designed upon a Substitution-Permutation Network (SPN):
- Block Size: Fixed 128 bits = 16 bytes.
- Key Size: 128 bits = 16 bytes ($N_k = 4$ 32-bit words).
- Number of Transformation Rounds: $N_r = 10$ rounds.
- State Matrix Representation: The 16-byte block is arranged into a $4 \times 4$ byte matrix in column-major order:
  $$\text{State}[r][c] = \text{input}[r + 4c], \quad 0 \le r, c \le 3$$

---

## 2. Galois Field Arithmetic on GF(2^8)
Byte-level operations are computed over the finite field $\text{GF}(2^8) \cong \mathbb{F}_2[x] / \langle m(x) \rangle$:
- Irreducible Polynomial:
  $$m(x) = x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x + 1 \quad (\text{hexadecimal } \mathtt{0x11B})$$
- Field Addition & Subtraction: Equivalent to bitwise XOR ($\oplus$).
- Polynomial Multiplication: Achieved via polynomial multiplication modulo $m(x)$.
- Optimized `xtime(a)` Function: Multiplies a field element by $x \equiv \mathtt{0x02}$:
  $$\text{xtime}(a) = \begin{cases} (a \ll 1) \oplus \mathtt{0x1B} & \text{if MSB of } a = 1 \\ (a \ll 1) & \text{if MSB of } a = 0 \end{cases} \pmod{2^8}$$
- General Field Multiplication `gf_mul(a, b)`: Implemented by decomposing multiplier $b$ into powers of $x$ and accumulating successive `xtime` doublings.

## 3. Four Core State Round Transformations
Rounds 1 through 9 execute four consecutive invertible transformations:

### 3.1. SubBytes and InvSubBytes (Non-linear Byte Substitution)
- Replaces each byte $s_{r,c}$ independently using the non-linear S-Box table:
  $$s'_{r,c} = \text{S\_BOX}[s_{r,c}]$$
- Mathematical Derivation: Multiplicative inverse in $\text{GF}(2^8)$ (mapping $\mathtt{0x00} \to \mathtt{0x00}$), followed by an affine transformation over $\text{GF}(2)$.
- Decryption employs $\text{INV\_S\_BOX}$: $s_{r,c} = \text{INV\_S\_BOX}[s'_{r,c}]$.

### 3.2. ShiftRows and InvShiftRows (Cyclic Row Permutation)
- Cyclically shifts rows to the left by distinct byte offsets:
  - Row 0: Unchanged (shift 0).
  - Row 1: Shift left by 1: $s'_{1, c} = s_{1, (c+1) \bmod 4}$.
  - Row 2: Shift left by 2: $s'_{2, c} = s_{2, (c+2) \bmod 4}$.
  - Row 3: Shift left by 3: $s'_{3, c} = s_{3, (c+3) \bmod 4}$.
- Decryption (InvShiftRows): Cyclically shifts rows to the right by corresponding offsets ($0, 1, 2, 3$).

### 3.3. MixColumns and InvMixColumns (Column Diffusion)
- Multiplies each state column by a fixed circulant polynomial $c(x) = \{03\}x^3 + \{01\}x^2 + \{01\}x + \{02\} \pmod{x^4 + 1}$:
  $$\begin{bmatrix} s'_{0,c} \\ s'_{1,c} \\ s'_{2,c} \\ s'_{3,c} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 02 & 03 & 01 & 01 \\ 01 & 02 & 03 & 01 \\ 01 & 01 & 02 & 03 \\ 03 & 01 & 01 & 02 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} s_{0,c} \\ s_{1,c} \\ s_{2,c} \\ s_{3,c} \end{bmatrix}$$
- Decryption (InvMixColumns): Multiplies by the inverse matrix with coefficients $\{0\text{e}, 0\text{b}, 0\text{d}, 09\}$:
  $$\begin{bmatrix} s_{0,c} \\ s_{1,c} \\ s_{2,c} \\ s_{3,c} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0\text{e} & 0\text{b} & 0\text{d} & 09 \\ 09 & 0\text{e} & 0\text{b} & 0\text{d} \\ 0\text{d} & 09 & 0\text{e} & 0\text{b} \\ 0\text{b} & 0\text{d} & 09 & 0\text{e} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} s'_{0,c} \\ s'_{1,c} \\ s'_{2,c} \\ s'_{3,c} \end{bmatrix}$$

### 3.4. AddRoundKey (Round Key XOR)
- Bitwise XOR between state bytes and the corresponding round subkey $K_r$:
  $$s'_{r,c} = s_{r,c} \oplus k_{r,c}$$

## 4. Key Expansion Algorithm
- The 16-byte master key $K$ forms words $w_0, w_1, w_2, w_3$.
- Successive words $w_4 \dots w_{43}$ are generated recursively:
  $$\text{temp} = w_{i-1}$$
  If $i \equiv 0 \pmod 4$:
  $$\text{temp} = \text{SubWord}(\text{RotWord}(\text{temp})) \oplus \text{Rcon}[i / 4]$$
  $$w_i = w_{i-4} \oplus \text{temp}$$
  Where:
  - `RotWord`: Left cyclic byte shift $[b_0, b_1, b_2, b_3] \to [b_1, b_2, b_3, b_0]$.
  - `SubWord`: Applies S-Box substitution to each byte.
  - `Rcon[j]`: Round constant with leading byte $x^{j-1}$ in $\text{GF}(2^8)$ and trailing zeros.
- Derives 11 round subkeys $K_0, K_1, \dots, K_{10}$ in $4 \times 4$ matrix format.

## 5. Cipher Block Chaining (CBC) Mode and PKCS#7 Padding
- **PKCS#7 Padding (RFC 5652):**
  If message length is not a multiple of 16, append $pad\_len = 16 - (\text{len}(P) \bmod 16)$ bytes, each equal to $pad\_len$. If length is already a multiple of 16, append a full 16-byte block of `0x10`.
- **CBC Encryption:**
  - $C_1 = E_K(P_1 \oplus \text{IV})$.
  - $C_i = E_K(P_i \oplus C_{i-1}), \quad i \ge 2$.
  - Output format: $\text{IV} \parallel C_1 \parallel C_2 \dots \parallel C_m$.
- **CBC Decryption:**
  - $P_1 = D_K(C_1) \oplus \text{IV}$.
  - $P_i = D_K(C_i) \oplus C_{i-1}, \quad i \ge 2$.
  - Strict validation and removal of PKCS#7 padding.

## 6. Vulnerability Analysis and Educational Scope
1. **Padding Oracle Attack:** Occurs when servers leak distinguishable responses between padding failures and decryption errors, allowing iterative plaintext recovery.
2. **Cache-Timing Side Channels:** Table lookups on `S_BOX` and `MixColumns` create variable cache access latencies. This implementation is educational (non-constant-time) and not for production.
3. **Empirical Throughput:** Student key `SHA-256("Nguyen Thi Nhu Y")[:16]` achieves 37,208 ops/s key expansion, ~0.28 MB/s CBC encryption, and ~0.027 MB/s CBC decryption.
